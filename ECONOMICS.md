# Economics

Assume average successful Credex conversion yields **$8,000 annual gross profit** (spread/margin from credit resale and retained account value). If this estimate is too high, adjust proportionally; the framework still holds.

## Lead value estimate
- Audit completed → consultation booked: 12%
- Consultation booked → credit purchase: 25%
- Purchase value to Credex: $8,000 gross profit/year

Expected value per completed audit:
`0.12 * 0.25 * 8000 = $240`

Expected value per captured lead (assuming lead capture rate from audits is 22%):
`$240 / 0.22 = ~$1,091`

## CAC by channel (from GTM)
- Founder DMs: mostly time cost; assume $40/hour loaded cost, 3 hours for 8 audits ⇒ ~$15 CAC per audit.
- Community posts/newsletters: near-zero cash, but content prep + follow-up; blended ~$10 CAC per audit.
- Referral loop from shared reports: near-zero marginal CAC after initial setup.

If expected value per audit is ~$240, paying $10–$20 equivalent CAC is strongly profitable.

## Profitability thresholds
Let:
- `A` = monthly completed audits
- `b` = consult booking rate
- `p` = purchase rate from consult
- `v` = gross profit per purchase

Monthly gross profit: `A * b * p * v`

For `A=1,000`, `b=10%`, `p=20%`, `v=$8,000`:
`1,000 * 0.1 * 0.2 * 8,000 = $160,000/month` gross profit run-rate.

## Path to $1M ARR in 18 months
To hit $1M ARR from this channel, annualized gross profit from converted deals must exceed ~$1M. A plausible route:
- 1,500 audits/month
- 10% consult booking
- 15% purchase conversion
- $4,500 annual gross profit per purchase

Annualized result:
`1,500 * 0.10 * 0.15 * 4,500 * 12 = $1,215,000`

The tool works if audit volume and downstream conversion stay consistent, and Credex can operationally fulfill demand fast.
